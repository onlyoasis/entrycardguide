The arrival card is one check. At the counter, the officer also reads your passport's expiry date and can ask how you will leave and how you will pay for the stay. Airlines check the passport rule at check-in as well.

This table covers all 53 destinations with a full guide on this site. Every rule is quoted from a government page: an immigration department, a foreign ministry, an official e-visa portal, an embassy or the law itself. The domain under each rule links to that page. Many rules cover one route only, such as an e-visa or visa-free entry. The label after a rule says which.

## How to count the months

Countries count in six ways. Use the dates on your ticket, not today's date.

- **On the day you arrive.** Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Egypt, Saudi Arabia, Sri Lanka, Kenya, Ethiopia, Laos, Peru and Cuba want 6 months left when you land, and so do Taiwan and India on some routes. Land on 1 March 2027 and the passport must still be valid on 1 September 2027.
- **After your stay ends.** The Philippines and the US count 6 months from the end of your planned stay, and the Bahamas 6 months from the day you leave. New Zealand counts 3 months and South Africa 30 days from the day you leave. Turkey adds 60 days to the stay your visa or exemption allows, so a 90-day stay needs 150 days left on arrival.
- **For your whole stay.** The UK, Japan, Korea, Canada, Mexico, Australia, Jamaica and Barbados only need the passport to be valid until you leave. Aruba and Curaçao say the same for visa-free visitors, and so does China for its 30-day visa exemption.
- **On the day you apply.** Russia's unified e-visa, Myanmar's tourist eVisa, Nepal's online visa and visas for Tanzania and Colombia need 6 months left when you submit the application. Morocco's eVisa needs 90 days.
- **Against the visa.** Vietnam wants the passport to expire at least 30 days after the visa. Georgia, Azerbaijan and Uzbekistan want it to outlast the visa by at least 3 months.
- **No start date given.** Singapore, the Dominican Republic, Jordan, Oman, Pakistan and Nigeria say 6 months, Panama 3 months and the Maldives 1 month, without saying from when. China asks visa applicants for 6 months and 240-hour transit passengers for 3 months, also with no start date. Count from the day you leave to be safe.

Exceptions are common. A Philippine immigration order lets 41 visa-free nationalities enter with less than 6 months, and passport agreements exempt many countries from the US rule. Canada goes the other way for visitor visa applicants: their passport should be valid for 6 months from the travel date. South Africa's new ETA asks for 7 months from the travel date. Each country row says who an exception covers.

## E-passports

An e-passport has a chip inside and the e-passport symbol on the cover. Seven destinations in the table have a chip rule:

- **United States.** The Visa Waiver Program (ESTA) only accepts e-passports. The requirement is in the law, 8 U.S.C. 1187.
- **Australia.** You apply for the ETA in the Australian ETA app, which reads the passport chip.
- **Japan.** Visa-free entry on Thai, Malaysian, Brazilian, UAE, Panamanian, Paraguayan, Peruvian, Serbian and Montenegrin passports needs an ICAO-standard e-passport. Indonesian e-passports must be registered with a Japanese embassy or consulate first.
- **Curaçao and Aruba.** Ukrainian citizens enter visa-free only with a biometric passport.
- **United Kingdom.** The UK ETA app scans the chip when it can and reads the details from a photo of the passport when it cannot. You do not need a chip.
- **Israel.** ETA-IL accepts both biometric and non-biometric passports.

For the other destinations we found no chip rule. Their official pages ask for a valid passport. A machine-readable passport is a different thing: the Maldives, South Africa and Russia's unified e-visa ask for one, and it does not need a chip.

## Return ticket and money

Most destinations ask for a ticket out. Thailand wants one leaving within 30 days of arrival. Malaysia, Indonesia, Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Qatar, Kenya, Tanzania, Cuba, Panama, Peru, Colombia and most of the Caribbean require one, and so do Taiwan, the Philippines and Oman on their visa-free routes. Japan, Mexico and Brazil say officers may ask for it, and so do Morocco for eVisa holders and South Africa for ETA holders. China advises visa-free visitors to carry their air tickets and hotel bookings. Australia says no return ticket is required, but officers still need to see that you can pay to leave. The Philippines' 14-day visa-free entry for Chinese nationals, in force since 16 January 2026, also needs a confirmed hotel booking and only works at Manila (NAIA) and Mactan-Cebu airports.

Some countries publish an amount. New Zealand asks for NZD 1,000 a month, or NZD 400 if your accommodation is paid. Thailand lists THB 20,000 per person, Panama at least PAB 500, and Aruba and Curaçao USD 150–200 a day in a hotel. Morocco's eVisa asks for an international bank card or more than EUR 70 in cash a day. Colombia's tourist visa needs a monthly income of at least 10 minimum wages. Most other countries ask for enough money for the stay and publish no number.

## What "No official rule found" means

We read the immigration, foreign-ministry and embassy pages for each country and quote what they say. "No official rule found" means those pages state no rule on that point. It does not stop an officer from asking. Rules change, and the official page linked in each row is the authority. The Checked column is the date we last read the rules for that country.

## Questions

### How many months must my passport be valid to travel?

It depends on the country. Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Egypt, Sri Lanka, Kenya, Peru and Cuba ask for 6 months left on the day you arrive. The Philippines and the US count 6 months from the end of your stay, and both exempt many nationalities. New Zealand wants 3 months after you leave and South Africa 30 days. Vietnam, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Uzbekistan count against the visa: the passport has to outlast it. The UK, Japan, Korea, Canada, Mexico, Australia, Jamaica and Barbados only need the passport to last for your stay, though Canada asks visitor visa applicants for 6 months from the travel date.

### Do I need an e-passport?

Only for some routes. The US Visa Waiver Program (ESTA) requires one by law. Australia's ETA is applied for in an app that reads the passport chip. Japan gives visa-free entry to Thai, Malaysian, Brazilian, UAE and five other passports only if they are ICAO-standard e-passports, and Indonesians must register theirs first. Curaçao and Aruba let Ukrainians in visa-free only with a biometric passport. The UK ETA app and Israel's ETA-IL work without a chip.

### How long must my passport be valid for China's 240-hour visa-free transit?

At least 3 months, according to the National Immigration Administration, which does not say which date to count from. You also need a connecting ticket with a fixed date to a third country or region, and you can only enter at the listed ports.

### Do I need a return ticket to enter Thailand?

Yes, under the tourist visa exemption. Thai embassies say you need a confirmed air, train, bus or boat ticket out of Thailand within 30 days of arrival. Without one, get a tourist visa first.

### How much money do I need to show in Thailand?

Thai embassy pages updated in September 2026 list THB 20,000 per person or THB 40,000 per family for the tourist visa exemption. The embassy in Wellington lists THB 10,000 and THB 20,000. Carry the higher amount.

### Can I enter Japan with a passport that expires soon?

Yes, if it stays valid for your whole stay. The Consulate-General of Japan in Seattle says Japan sets no minimum remaining validity. Leave one blank page for the entry stamp.

### What does "No official rule found" mean?

The immigration, foreign-ministry and embassy pages we read state no rule on that point. It does not stop an officer from asking. The official page linked in each row is the authority.
