## Start from the government page

{{< official-link site="saudi-arabia.national_platform" >}}

The tourist eVisa application does not live on a `.gov.sa` host. That is worth stating plainly rather than glossing over: the portal calls itself the official tourist eVisa portal, and its terms page says the service is operated by the **Ministry of Tourism**, but the hostname itself carries no government suffix.

The way to check that you are in the right place is to arrive from a government page and then read the address bar.

{{< official-link site="saudi-arabia.tourist_evisa" >}}

The older `visa.mofa.gov.sa` address is not the clearest current entry point for the ordinary eligible-country tourist eVisa.

## Who can use it

**66 eligible-country passports** can apply for the tourist eVisa.

If your passport is not on that list, the route is different, not closed.

{{< official-link site="saudi-arabia.visa_finder" >}}

Holders of specified valid **US, UK or Schengen visas or permanent residence**, and **GCC residents**, must use the finder, because their eligibility is decided on different terms from the 66-country list. Transit visas, Umrah and Nusuk permits, Hajj visas and GCC-resident routes are separate products.

{{< official-link site="saudi-arabia.ksa_visa" >}}

## What it costs

The tourist visa base government fee is **SAR 300**.

That is the base, not the total. Mandatory **medical insurance**, **VAT** and the payment amount all affect what you are charged at checkout. Budgeting SAR 300 flat and then meeting a larger number is the most common surprise in this flow, and it is not a middleman markup.

## What the visa covers

{{< official-link site="saudi-arabia.terms" >}}

The tourist visa covers **tourism, events, family and relative visits, leisure, and Umrah excluding Hajj**. It does not cover **Hajj, study or work**.

{{< official-link site="saudi-arabia.umrah_route" >}}

The Umrah visa is applied for through KSA Visa, and the Umrah permit through Nusuk. Hajj requires its own visa and permit. A tourist eVisa does not stand in for either.

## Validity

The eVisa is normally valid for **one year**, allows **multiple entries**, and permits stays of up to **90 days**. The passport needs at least **6 months** of validity at entry.

A second visit in the same year usually needs no second visa. Check the validity on the issued document before buying again.
