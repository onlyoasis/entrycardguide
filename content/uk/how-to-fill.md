## What does “provide the address where you live most of the time” mean? {#home-address}

It means the place you normally live now. For example, if you live in Canada and are visiting London for a week, use your home in Canada for this question, rather than the London hotel. Do not substitute a workplace or a relative's address simply because it is easier to enter.

This is our plain-English reading of the question. The Home Office's {{< source-link site="uk.application_help" text="application guidance" >}} confirms that the ETA asks about your address, but does not explain how to choose between several homes. **Source checked: 17 October 2026.** If you split your time between homes or have no settled address, use the on-screen help or {{< source-link site="uk.eta_support" text="ask UKVI about your situation" >}} before guessing.

## What goes in UK ETA address line 1? {#address-line-1}

Start with the part that identifies your home, such as its house number and street or its building name. Put the rest in the matching boxes that your screen provides. Keep the flat or unit number somewhere in the address so it still identifies your home.

**These examples are fictional.** They illustrate an address split, not a Home Office requirement or a promise that the app will accept that exact format. Do not copy them into your application.

| Box shown on your screen | Fictional house example | Fictional flat example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Address line 1 | 12 Example Road | 24 Sample Street |
| Address line 2, if provided | Leave empty if optional and there is nothing to add | Flat 4 |
| Town or city, if separate | Ottawa | Ottawa |
| Province or region, if requested | Ontario | Ontario |
| Postal code, if requested | Use your own home's actual code | Use your own home's actual code |
| Country | Canada | Canada |

If there is no additional address line, include the unit with the street address, for example **Flat 4, 24 Sample Street**. If your home has a building name rather than a street number, use its actual identifying details. Do not invent a house number or a UK postcode to imitate the examples.

**Source boundary, checked 17 October 2026:** the {{< source-link site="uk.application_help" text="public Home Office app guide" >}} does not publish address-line character limits, accepted punctuation or a format for every country. The examples above are editorial guidance. Follow the labels, required-field markers and help on your live application. [See the home-address field card](#field-home_address) for the short version.

## “Enter address line 1”: errors and app problems {#address-errors}

If you see **“Enter address line 1”**, start by checking the first address box itself. That wording alone does not establish why the application has rejected the page.

### Checks you can try before continuing

These are basic input checks suggested by this guide, **not verified fixes for a UK ETA software bug**:

1. Check that the first line contains your home address, rather than only an entry in line 2. If autofill put text in the wrong box, put it in the matching box.
2. If line 1 looks filled but the prompt remains, try typing the same truthful address manually. Check for an accidentally pasted line break or a field containing only spaces. This may help you spot an input mistake; it is not a confirmed fix.
3. Read the whole error message and any field-level hint. If the live form gives a length or format rule, follow that rule while keeping your address accurate. We have not verified a universal ETA rule requiring you to remove punctuation, accents or spaces.
4. Check the country, town and postal-code boxes against your actual home address where those boxes are shown. Do not replace your home with a UK hotel or enter a made-up postal code to get past an error. If your address has no postal code and the form blocks you, ask UKVI what to enter.

### Official options if the app still will not continue

The Home Office says you can {{< source-link site="uk.eta_online" text="apply online instead" >}} if you have problems using the app. That is an official alternative, not a guaranteed address-error fix. The guidance does not promise that an unfinished app application will transfer to the browser.

You can also {{< source-link site="uk.eta_support" text="use the official ETA webchat" >}}. Note the exact error wording, whether you used the app or browser, your device and app version, and the step where it stopped. Use official support for application details; do not post your passport or full home address in public comments.

**Official troubleshooting source checked: 17 October 2026:** {{< source-link site="uk.application_help" text="Using the UK ETA app, “If you need help using the app”" >}}. No address-specific bug or guaranteed workaround was verified for this guide. If you already paid or submitted, check your confirmation and ask official support before starting another application just to troubleshoot.

Use the passport you will carry. Apply through GOV.UK or the UK ETA app published by the Home Office. The official fee is £20 for each person, including every baby and child. There is no family discount.

## Before payment {#before-payment}

Check that the browser route is on `gov.uk`, or that the UK ETA app lists **Home Office** as its developer or seller. The government charge is £20. A higher total includes a commercial service fee.

Use an email address you control. Most decisions arrive within a day, but allow up to 3 working days. Save the decision email and 16-digit reference number.

The ETA is linked to the passport used in the application. It lasts 2 years or until that passport expires, whichever comes first. A new passport requires a new ETA.
