## Quick answer

Air Suvidha 2.0 is India's **free** health self-declaration for international arrivals. A June 2026 advisory from the Directorate General of Health Services made it required because of the Ebola outbreak. Fill it at **airsuvidha.civilaviation.gov.in** up to **72 hours before arriving in India**.

Everyone arriving on an international flight files it: Indian and foreign nationals, airline crew and infants. Foreign nationals also file the separate [e-Arrival Card](/india/e-arrival-card/).

## India has two free forms

The two forms are run by different ministries on different sites. Filing one does not file the other.

A foreign tourist landing in Delhi therefore files two forms, both free: the e-Arrival Card, plus Air Suvidha. An Indian citizen returning home files Air Suvidha only.

## Who has to file it

From the official FAQ, read 18 November 2026:

- **Must file:** "All international travellers (Indian and Foreign nationals) and airline crew arriving in India".
- **OCI cardholders:** yes. Choose the Foreigner tab, your passport nationality, and "OCI Card Holder Visa" as the visa type.
- **Infants and children:** infants need their own form. Children may use a parent's email.
- **Transit:** needed if you clear immigration in India. Staying airside to change planes is not covered by the FAQ's wording.
- **Not needed:** passengers departing India.

## The 72-hour window

The form says: "Passenger can fill the application up to 72 hrs before arriving in India." Arrival date and time are in Indian time (IST). On 18 November 2026 the date picker offered 18 to 21 November.

Example: landing in Mumbai at 02:00 IST on 21 November, the form opens around 02:00 IST on 18 November. The FAQ recommends filing with web check-in, and the common-mistakes list warns against leaving it until the last minute because of OTP delays.

## What the form asks

Four steps, from the official instructions:

1. **Personal and flight details:** travelling alone or as a family (up to 4 family members per submission, each gets their own declaration), full name, gender, age, nationality, passport number, Ebola-affected countries visited or transited in the last 21 days (DR Congo, Uganda, South Sudan; the form also lists Kenya), first country of boarding and departure date, first airport of arrival in India, airline, flight number, seat (optional), arrival date and time, onward travel, and each state and district you will visit.
2. **Contact and OTP:** address in India (or foreign stay details), email, mobile. An Indian number gets the OTP by SMS and email; any other number gets it by email only.
3. **Health declaration:** shown only if you declared travel to an Ebola-affected country.
4. **Consent and submit.**

Select the airport where you first land and clear immigration, not your final city. Declare a transit through a listed country even if you never left the terminal.

## Mistakes and changes

- **Edit until arrival:** log in with the OTP sent to your registered email. After you arrive, corrections go through the Airport Port Health Officer (APHO) health desk.
- **New flight:** if the itinerary changes significantly, submit a new form.
- **Wrong passport number:** you cannot travel on that form. Correct it in the edit window or file again.
- **Use your own contact details:** the FAQ says not to give a travel agent's phone or email. The OTP for any later edit goes there.

## Paid filing services

The FAQ answers this directly: "The official Air Suvidha service is completely free." It tells travellers to avoid third-party websites or agents that ask for money.

Agencies sell it anyway. On 12 August 2026 a US visa-service company announced Air Suvidha filing as a flat $39 add-on to its India visa orders (read 18 November 2026). That money buys typing, not a different form. Fill it yourself on the `.gov.in` site in about ten minutes.
